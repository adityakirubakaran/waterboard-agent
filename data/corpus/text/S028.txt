Introduction
The State Water Resources Control Board’s (State Water Board) Drinking Water Operator Certification requirements ensure that customers of any public water system are provided with adequate supply of safe, potable drinking water, consumers are confident that their water is safe to drink, public water system operators are trained and certified, and that they have knowledge and understanding of the public health reasons for drinking water standards. To further these goals, the State Water Board has coordinated with the Drinking Water Operator Certification Advisory Committee, the Operator Experience Guidance Sub Workgroup, and stakeholders to develop the Drinking Water Operator Certification Program (DWOCP), Distribution Operator and Water Treatment Operator Certification Application Guidance (Guidance) to assist applicants in applying for drinking water operator certification.

[page 3] Operator Certification Requirements
Any person operating a water distribution system must hold a valid, unexpired water distribution operator certificate. “Operates a water distribution system” means actions or decisions to control the quality or quantity of drinking water in a water distribution system, including: 1) supervision of other persons operating a water distribution system;
and 2) all activities listed in subdivisions (b) through (d) of section 63770 of the DWOCP Regulations, as an activity that may only be performed by a person with a water

To be certified as a water distribution operator or water treatment operator, an applicant must meet all the following:
• Minimum education requirements to take the examination for each applicable grade level and pass the examination. The amount of education required increases with each grade level.
• Minimum experience requirements for each applicable grade level. The amount of operator experience required increases with each grade level.
Chief and Shift Operator Requirements
A “chief operator” is the person who has overall responsibility for the day-to-day, hands-on, operation of a water treatment plant or the person who has overall responsibility for the day-to-day, hands-on, operation of a water distribution system. A “shift operator” is the person in direct charge of the operation of a water treatment plant or water distribution system for a specified period of the day.

Operator Experience Requirements
Applicants for a water distribution operator certificate or water treatment operator certificate at the grade 3 level and above must meet minimum on-the-job operator experience requirements. The amount of operator experience required increases with each grade level.
Experience can be obtained by operating a water distribution system or water treatment plant while holding the applicable certificate. Specifically, “operator experience” means the daily performance of activities consisting of the control or oversight of any process or operation of a water treatment plant or in a water distribution system that may affect the quality or quantity of water.
There are two required categories of operator experience:

[page 8] There are two required categories of operator experience:
• Time-in-Grade Experience – experience gained by an operator with a certificate at the specified grade level while operating a water distribution system or water treatment plant that has a classification equivalent to that specified grade level or higher. For example, the eligibility criteria for water treatment operator certification at the grade 4 level requires at least one year of operator experience working as a shift or chief operator, while holding a valid water treatment operator certificate at the grade 3 level, at a water treatment plant classified as a level 3 facility or higher. Water treatment operator experience gained at a lower grade or facility classification will not meet this requirement.
• Additional years of operator experience – experience gained by a certified operator while operating any classification of water treatment plant or water distribution system.

[page 8] • Additional years of operator experience – experience gained by a certified operator while operating any classification of water treatment plant or water distribution system.
There also are some provisions in the DWOCP Regulations that allow an applicant to meet specified experience requirements through obtaining other types of experience or additional education. For more information on these provisions, refer to sections 63800 and 63805 of the DWOCP Regulations and appendices I and II.
Evaluation of Operator Experience Credit
Generally, the State Water Board will award certification applicants who work full-time 40 hours per week of operator experience and will award certification applicants who work part-time a prorated amount of operator experience. When certification applicants work in both disciplines (water distribution operator and water treatment operator), the hours per week of operator experience must be divided between both disciplines, not counted for both, and clearly stated on each supervisor letter.

[page 8] Generally, the State Water Board will award no more than 40 hours per week of operator experience. It should be noted, however, that certification applicants who have been designated a chief operator or a shift operator may be awarded additional operator

Reinstatement

[page 11] Reinstatement
If the applicable continuing education contact hours are not completed by the certificate expiration date, the operator has a six-month grace period to complete them. During this grace period, the certification is expired and the operator cannot work in operations until the certification has been renewed. If an operator completes the applicable continuing education contact hours within six months of the certification’s expiration, the operator is eligible to apply for certification renewal for up to one year after the certification expiration date. Certifications that are not renewed within one year of the certification expiration date no longer can be renewed, and the individual must take and pass an examination to be eligible for a new certification. For more information on these provisions, refer to section 63845 of the DWOCP Regulations.
Reciprocity
The State Water Board may grant certification through reciprocity.

[page 11] The State Water Board may grant certification through reciprocity.
To be eligible for certification through reciprocity, an applicant must hold a valid, unexpired, comparable water distribution operator certificate or water treatment operator certificate issued by another State, the United States, a territory, or tribal government that has been designated as the primacy agency by the United States Environmental Protection Agency, or a unit of any of these. An applicant’s education and experience must be equitable to the California requirements.

Enforcement
Any person operating a water distribution system, water treatment plant, or water recycling treatment plant without an appropriate, valid, unexpired certification is guilty of a misdemeanor and may be liable civilly. Owners or permittees and supervising operators, including chief operators, who permit a person who does not have an appropriate, valid, unexpired certification to operate a water distribution system, water treatment plant, or water recycling treatment plant also may be liable for permitting this violation.
An applicant who submits false or misleading information on an application for certification or an examination is guilty of a misdemeanor and may be liable civilly.
Supervising operators, including chief operators, who sign false or misleading supervisor letters in support of an applicant’s application for certification also may be guilty of a misdemeanor and may be liable civilly.

[page 12] Any person, or government agency may file an allegation with the State Water Board, Office of Enforcement. Complaints may be submitted via email directly to the Office of Enforcement, Special Investigators Unit at SIU@waterboards.ca.gov. For additional information you may also visit the complaints webpage, https://www.waterboards.ca.gov/water_issues/programs/operator_certification/complaint .html.
The State Water Board may suspend, revoke, or refuse to grant or renew any water distribution operator certificate or may place on probation or reprimand a water distribution certificate holder upon any reasonable grounds, including, but not limited to, any of the reasons set forth in subdivision (b) of Health and Safety Code section 106877. Any water distribution operator who commits specified acts set forth in Health and Safety Code section 106878 also is guilty of a misdemeanor and may be liable civilly.

[page 12] The State Water Board may suspend, revoke, or refuse to grant or renew any water treatment operator certificate or may place on probation or reprimand a water treatment certificate holder upon any reasonable grounds, including, but not limited to any of the reasons set forth in subdivision (a) of Health and Safety Code section 106877. Any water treatment operator who commits specified acts set forth in Health and Safety Code section 106878 also is guilty of a misdemeanor and may be liable civilly. In addition, any water treatment operator who operates a water recycling plant may be subject to discipline or civil liability under the statutes and regulations applicable to Wastewater Operator Certification.
